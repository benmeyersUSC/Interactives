    The argument

    Pulling arm k pays a reward of 1 with probability μk and 0 otherwise. The best mean is μ*, and arm k falls short of it by the gap Δk. Regret counts, round by round, how much mean reward the pulled arm gave up. Every pull of arm k gives up exactly Δk, so the sum over rounds in plot 03 must equal the gaps times the counts in the pull strip. The checks confirm the two routes agree.

    Hoeffding bounds how far an average of N rewards in [0, 1] strays from its mean. A run can look at K arms, and each arm at up to T different counts, so there are at most K·T intervals it will ever read. Giving each a failure budget of δ/(KT) makes all of them hold together with probability at least 1 − δ: the good event. The price is L = ln(2KT/δ) in place of ln(2/δ), which widens every interval by the square root of their ratio. The lecture form, √(ln(KT/δ)/N), is the same idea with a looser constant: it is about √2 wider and holds with probability at least 1 − 2δ²/(KT).

    On the good event the best arm's upper end never falls below μ*. A worse arm's upper end is at most its true mean plus twice its width. So a worse arm can only win while twice its width exceeds its gap, which caps its count at about 2L/Δk². In plot 05 the cap is where the width law crosses Δk/2. Summing gap times cap gives regret that grows like log T, while pulling arms uniformly grows linearly in t.

    UCB1 (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi and Fischer, 2002) uses √(2 ln t / N) and needs no horizon; its caps grow like 8 ln t/Δ². The forgetful rule sizes each interval for one reading and loses the guarantee for the thousands it actually reads. Hoeffding is loose for coin flips, so this usually goes unpunished. On the default arms over 200 seeds at δ = 0.05, it had median regret 20 against the union bound's 57, but missed in 26 runs where the union bound missed in none. At δ = 0.5 it missed in 140 runs, and its worst run paid 318 because an unlucky start starved the best arm; the union bound's worst was 91. The union bound is insurance, and plot 04 shows its premium.

    Assumptions and how the plots are drawn

    • Rewards are Bernoulli. Each arm draws from its own seeded stream (mulberry32), so the j-th pull of arm k returns the same reward whatever the rule, δ or horizon. Switch the rule on the same seed to compare fairly; finished runs stay in plot 04 as faint curves while the arms are unchanged.
    • Unpulled arms have upper end ∞ and ties go to the lowest arm, so rounds 1 to K pull each arm once. For UCB1, t in ln t is counted from K.
    • Regret is pseudo-regret: it uses the true means, not the rewards that happened to land. The good event is checked after every round, for every arm, with the widths the next round will use.
    • Plot 01's vertical axis always contains every finite interval and the band [0, 1]. It rescales only when an end would leave the frame or the intervals fill less than about half of it, in rounded steps, easing in, so a single round shows shrinking and not rescaling.
    • One precision per quantity everywhere on the page: true means 2 decimals; estimates, widths and ends 3; regret, L and gaps 2. In the index cascade the estimate appears as the exact fraction S/N and the width with the exact settings, and the last line shows ≈ when adding the rounded terms would print a different index.
    • Plot 04's dashed curve is the most regret any run could have by round t if every interval held: the first pulls, then the largest gaps filled up to their caps. The dotted line is the expected regret of pulling arms uniformly at random.
    • Plot 02 draws expected rewards for all three strategies, so the gap between the optimal line and UCB's line is exactly the regret of plot 04; the faint jagged line is the rewards UCB actually collected, which scatter around its expectation.
    • Plots 02, 03 and 04 share a round axis that grows in small rounded steps: when the newest round passes about 60% of the window, the window grows to about twice the newest round, so the newest point sits near the middle until the horizon. Plot 04's regret axis grows the same way.
